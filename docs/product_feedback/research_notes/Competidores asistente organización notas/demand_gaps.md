# User demand, unmet needs and market gaps in organizing personal notes with AI (as of 2026-11-09)

Coverage limits that apply to every section:
- **Reddit was not read.** The search and fetch tools refuse reddit.com, so no r/secondbrain, r/PKMS, r/Notion or r/ObsidianMD thread was reviewed. Community evidence comes from the Obsidian forum, Tildes, Hacker News and MPU Talk, which skew technical. This is a material hole.
- Evidence labels: **[Peer-reviewed]**, **[Preprint]**, **[Vendor]** (sells a related product), **[Practitioner]** (individual essay), **[Anecdote]** (forum post or review), **[Market report]**.
- "Excerpt only" means I saw the text in search results but did not read the full source.

## How often do people report organizing as their main pain, compared with capture, retrieval or use?

### Takeaway
I found no survey that ranks these stages, so the claim that organizing is the main pain has no quantitative support and no quantitative refutation. The best recent qualitative study appears to point at retrieval and scattered information, while prominent essays point at use; organizing appears as a real but not clearly dominant complaint.

### Cited Findings
- **[Peer-reviewed, qualitative, n=27]** ECIS 2026 (June 2026): 27 semi-structured interviews with experienced knowledge workers yielded 50 first-order PKM challenges, grouped into 10 themes and 4 dimensions (cognitive, behavioral, technical, information-related), and mapped to 4 knowledge-management processes. Its framing is struggling to manage, retrieve and apply knowledge across scattered digital environments — [Korn et al., ECIS 2026](https://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2026/cog_hbis/cog_hbis/38/). Sample is experienced knowledge workers, not second-brain users; interview counts do not measure prevalence.
- **Same paper, excerpt only (full text returned 403):** memory decay is described as the most prevalent cognitive theme, primarily affecting retrieval; repository complexity is said to mainly affect storage and retrieval; storage mechanisms are said to determine retrieval capabilities — [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404434128_Mind_the_Mess_A_Matrix_of_Personal_Knowledge_Management_Challenges_Across_the_Knowledge_Lifecycle). Verify against the [PDF](https://aisel.aisnet.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1105&context=ecis2026) before citing publicly.
- **[Preprint, small and narrow]** IBM Research / PUC Minas case study (PDF dated September 2025): one-hour interviews with observation tasks, among Computer Science researchers known to the authors who use Obsidian. Key finding: the retrieval strategy people intend to use shapes how they create and organize notes. The authors acknowledge the narrow profile — [Ferreira et al., arXiv 2509.20187](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2509.20187). I did not locate the exact participant count in the text I extracted.
- **[Vendor survey, self-selected, excerpt only]** sipsip polled 1,200 of its own users; 68% said audio/video was more than half of their professional learning input and fewer than 20% had a capture system for it before sipsip. This concerns capture of media, not organizing, and the sample is the vendor's own customers — [sipsip](https://sipsip.ai/blog/personal-knowledge-management-best-practices-2026).
- **[Practitioner, December 2023, no data]** A challenge list leads with the "Placement Problem" and "Categorization Conundrum", then covers connection, attention, and retrieval and recall. It reports no survey or percentages — [Extended Brain](https://extendedbrain.substack.com/p/the-common-challenges-in-personal).
- **[Practitioner, August 2023, older]** Casey Newton's complaint is that note apps store information but do not improve thinking. Excerpt only; the sentiment drew responses mostly agreeing with the premise — [Platformer](https://platformer.substack.com/p/why-note-taking-apps-dont-make-us), [MPU Talk thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/casey-newton-why-note-taking-apps-dont-make-us-smarter/34611).
- **[Practitioner, June 2025]** Joan Westenberg deleted about 10,000 notes built over 7 years. The stated problem is use and thinking, not filing: "The more my system grew, the more I deferred the work of thought" and "A structure is not thinking. A tag is not an insight." — [Westenberg](https://westenberg.beehiiv.com/p/i-deleted-my-second-brain).
- **[Anecdotes, June–July 2025]** In the Tildes discussion of that essay, the dominant view is that writing helps thinking more than archiving does; some defend archives as reference ("I need my notes more than ever") — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1oth/i_deleted_my_second_brain).

### Inferences
- The team cannot honestly say that organizing is "the main problem for the majority of users". Neither its three Reddit responses nor anything found here supports a majority claim. The supportable wording is "a recurring pain for some users".
- The pain may be misfiled. Users may describe a placement problem while the cost they feel is failing to find or use notes. Ferreira et al. supports treating organizing and retrieval as linked, not as separate stages.
- Essays about not using notes do not contradict the team's failure to find demand for Express. Those authors mostly respond by simplifying or quitting, not by seeking a tool to generate output. But they do contradict the idea that organizing is where systems fail for this vocal group. Published critics are also a biased sample of articulate, heavy users.

### Gaps
- No representative survey or community poll ranking capture, organizing, retrieval and use was found for 2025–2026. Reddit polls may exist but could not be accessed.
- A short survey by the team, even in one community, would be new evidence and would beat most of what is publicly available. It would still be self-selected.

## Is organizing still a problem now that AI search and chat exist?

### Takeaway
"Just search" is a serious position with real evidence behind it, but that evidence is from email and predates LLMs, while the strongest current claims come from vendors with no data. No study was found that tests AI chat retrieval against organized notes, so this question is open and is the largest risk to the MVP.

### Cited Findings
Evidence that organizing does not matter:
- **[Peer-reviewed, 2011, email only, pre-LLM]** IBM study of 345 users and 85,000 refinding episodes: people who did not organize found emails faster than those who filed, were as successful, and saved the filing time — [MIT Technology Review on Whittaker et al.](https://www.technologyreview.com/2011/05/20/194578/stop-organizing-your-e-mail-says-study/). I read the coverage, not the paper. Email is not a notes collection, and the emails were written by others.
- **[Vendor, undated, no data]** Mem: "Folders are a human workaround for bad search. AI search makes them obsolete." It cites no studies, only patterns it says it sees among its users — [Mem](https://get.mem.ai/guides/why-folders-fail-ai-organization).
- **[Vendor, updated September 2026, no data]** "You no longer need to perfectly file a note into the correct PARA folder if an AI can search your entire workspace" — [Cleve](https://www.cleve.ai/blog/building-a-second-brain-summary).
- **[Vendor, July 2026, no data]** "A system that only stores is an expensive hard drive with a nice UI"; 2026 tools are said not to compete on folder taxonomy — [Arvor](https://arvor.co/en/news/second-brain-ai-2026).
- **[Anecdote, June 2025]** One commenter skips organizing altogether: "I can just use the search feature" — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1oth/i_deleted_my_second_brain).

Evidence that organizing still matters:
- **[Peer-reviewed, 2010, pre-LLM, local files]** 296 participants, mostly from Sheffield University, retrieved 1,131 of their own files. Navigation succeeded 94% of the time in 14.76 seconds on average, with shallow folders (mean depth 2.86). The authors call folder navigation the main retrieval method for personal files — [Bergman et al., JASIST preprint](https://www.tau.ac.il/education/muse/publications/101.pdf).
- **[Peer-reviewed, excerpt only, pre-LLM]** Users estimated they navigated for 56–68% of file retrievals and searched for 4–15%, whatever the search engine, with search as a last resort — [Bergman et al., "Improved search engines and navigation preference"](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1360855568725033728). The page returned no text when fetched, and the figures are self-estimates.
- **[Practitioner, June 2023, older, sells competing method]** After years of use, a Zettelkasten author says search "becomes less reliable and more cumbersome" as a collection grows. This is keyword-era personal experience with no data — [zettelkasten.de](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/building-a-second-brain-and-zettelkasten/).
- **[Anecdotes, March–September 2026]** Obsidian users are building structure for AI, not abandoning it: "treat AI sessions as stateless workers and the vault as the persistent state layer". No reply says structure is unnecessary. But others report that context files "silently go stale" and "Manual discipline at the end of a session almost always fails long-term" — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/design-your-vault-for-ai-orientation-not-just-human-navigation/112010). This is a self-selected, technical group.
- **[Vendor behavior, undated]** Mem, despite its "folders are obsolete" position, keeps collections in 2.0 and says tagging with them "has always been the easiest way to organize the way you think" (excerpt only) — [Mem 2.0 transition guide](https://get.mem.ai/blog/mem-2-dot-0-transition-guide). Product Hunt lists Mem 1.0 as an app that "kept you organized" and 2.0 as an "AI Thought Partner" (excerpt only) — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/mem-2). An opinion piece says that users of a system without structure lost context and trust; that page now returns 404, so this is unverified — [myICOR](https://blog.myicor.com/ai-note-taking-is-mem-20-the-next-big-thing).

### Inferences
- Pure "organize for its own sake" is a weak position. A tool selling tidier folders competes against a credible "you don't need folders" story and a behavior people can adopt for free.
- The stronger framing is organizing in service of retrieval and of AI context. Structure is valuable because it makes retrieval and agents work better, and upkeep is what fails. This framing is an inference from anecdotes and needs testing.
- Old folder studies show habit and adequate navigation, not that AI cannot replace it. Citing Bergman as proof against AI search would be overreach, as would citing Whittaker as proof for it.
- Mem is the closest natural experiment: a product built around automatic organization that later repositioned around a thought partner. The cause of that change is not established by what I read.

### Gaps
- No controlled or independent comparison of AI retrieval over messy versus organized notes was found. The team could cheaply run one on a beta tester's own Notion workspace; this would directly test the core assumption.
- No usage data on how often people use Notion AI, Mem or Obsidian AI plugins for retrieval rather than organizing.

## Why do people abandon second brain systems, do they trust automatic organizing, and what is said about PARA?

### Takeaway
Reported abandonment centers on maintenance overhead and a system that replaces real work, so automation could help but cannot fix the second cause. Evidence on trust is thin: there is real interest, a stated desire to shape one's own structure, and a suggest-then-confirm design pattern, yet no measurement. PARA is criticized for upkeep and boundary ambiguity, though even some quitters keep it.

### Cited Findings
Abandonment:
- **[Practitioner, November 2025]** Jack Allan Hyde gives three reasons: hoarding unread material, time lost to linking, tagging and plugins that felt productive without results, and links that remove accidental connections. Hyde still uses Obsidian as "a convenient note-taking app" — [Sudo Science](https://sudoscience.blog/2025/11/08/why-im-giving-up-on-a-second-brain/).
- **[Practitioner, June 2025]** Westenberg says that capture and filing deferred thinking and turned reading into extraction — [Westenberg](https://westenberg.beehiiv.com/p/i-deleted-my-second-brain).
- **[Anecdotes, June 2025]** One commenter warned that system tweaking can become "knowledge management porn"; others delete notes on purpose because ideas go stale — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1oth/i_deleted_my_second_brain).
- Survivorship caveat: these accounts describe people who write publicly about quitting. I found no abandonment rate.

Trust and control:
- **[Preprint, narrow sample]** Participants wanted to create their own structure, but a blank page could be overwhelming; structure changes constantly. The authors suggest a basic starting structure, changeable at any time — [Ferreira et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2509.20187). The authors note AI was not a focus of their interviews, so these design ideas are not user-tested AI preferences.
- **[Anecdotes, June 2025] Interest in automation:** one commenter says AI "excels at categorization" and wants handwritten notes indexed; another wants an assistant that can "remember stuff" — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1oth/i_deleted_my_second_brain).
- **[Anecdote, older forum thread, excerpt only] Manual confirmation in practice:** one user pastes notes into ChatGPT for tags, then adds them by hand — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/using-ai-to-create-suggest-tags/83853).
- **[Maker claim, excerpt only]** Vault Intelligence markets a Gardener agent that suggests ontology while keeping users "in control", and "Trust but Verify" confirmation for changes — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/vault-intelligence-active-reasoning-for-your-vault/110675). This shows what a builder thinks is required, not what users validated.
- **[Anecdotes, excerpt only]** AWS Marketplace reviewers report that Notion AI can be useful for finding and tidying, but also inconsistent or inaccurate — [AWS Marketplace reviews](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-eoc4gcvwcs3a6?page=734). The search result grouped several review pages; individual quotes were not matched to one page.
- **[Anecdote/third-party reviews, excerpt only]** AI organization in Mem is described as opaque when users want to file manually, and structured reference material as suffering under self-organization — [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/mem/review), [DEV Community](https://dev.to/pickuma/mem-review-ai-organized-notes-one-year-on-4h83) (the DEV page returned 404 when fetched). Quality of these review sites is low.

PARA:
- **[Practitioner, November 2025]** "PARA is fine." Hyde keeps it after dropping CODE, while complaining that it is unclear whether something belongs in Projects, Area or Reference — [Sudo Science](https://sudoscience.blog/2025/11/08/why-im-giving-up-on-a-second-brain/). This closely matches the team's Reddit user who cannot decide where to put notes.
- **[Vendor, July 2026]** PARA needs a "weekly move-down pass"; a skipped month lets finished work pile up in Projects. Some items sit on the Projects-Areas line, Resources can become a junk drawer, and "On its own, PARA is a filing cabinet". The author sells an AI second-brain service, and his claim that AI can do this upkeep is unverified — [Iwo Szapar](https://www.iwoszapar.com/p/para-method).
- **[Practitioner, June 2023, older, sells competing method]** PARA is "a production system" with no fixed location for notes: fine for small projects but not long-running research — [zettelkasten.de](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/building-a-second-brain-and-zettelkasten/).

### Inferences
- PARA's specific weaknesses (boundary ambiguity, stale Projects, Resources junk drawer) are concrete jobs that an assistant could do. This supports the team's direction more than the general claim about organizing does.
- The hazard is that a product may industrialize the very behavior critics blame. It can make systems look healthier while nobody uses the notes, and an abandoner's core reason would remain untouched.
- The safer default is suggestions the user approves, with undo, and no silent rewriting. The evidence for this is design convention plus anecdote, so it should be tested with beta users rather than presented as a finding.
- The team's third Reddit user (simple files, warns about overengineering) is not an outlier. Similar views recur in the Hyde, Westenberg and Tildes material. This segment is probably not a customer.

### Gaps
- No measured abandonment rate or time-to-abandon was found.
- No study was found on acceptance rates for AI organizing suggestions in notes, or on whether users prefer automatic or approved changes.
- No independent data was found on how many second-brain users actually follow PARA.

## What do people pay for PKM and note tools, and how large and fast-growing is the market?

### Takeaway
There is real money in the category, but most of the credible signal comes from Notion's team and enterprise business, not individuals organizing personal notes. Market-size reports differ by nearly threefold and should not anchor a pitch; no credible figure for the second-brain segment was found.

### Cited Findings
Prices, read from vendor pages on 2026-11-09:
- **Notion:** Free €0; Plus €9.50 per member/month; Business €19.50; Enterprise custom. Free and Plus get only a trial of Notion AI and limited trials of Notion Agent. Business includes Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes and Enterprise Search (beta). Custom Agents cost $10 per 1,000 monthly credits after trial — [Notion pricing](https://www.notion.com/pricing). Prices shown in EUR.
- **Obsidian:** core app free; Sync $4–5/user/month; Publish $8–10/site/month; commercial license $50/user/year — [Obsidian pricing](https://obsidian.md/pricing).
- **Mem:** Free; Plus $9; Pro $29; up to Pro 20× at $199/month; metered on messages and new notes (for example Plus has 50 and 50) — [Mem pricing](https://get.mem.ai/pricing).

Company scale (Notion and Obsidian do not publish audited numbers):
- **Notion valuation (primary):** about $11B in a roughly $270M tender, January 2026. More than 50% of ARR at end-2025 came from "AI-enabled customers", a figure that does not mean AI revenue — [Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/gic-sequoia-index-purchase-notion-shares).
- **Notion ARR (figures conflict):** $500M in 2025 and half of business/enterprise customers paying for AI, up from 10–20%; 100M+ users over its decade — [CNBC, May 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/notion-cnbc-disruptor-50-ranking.html). Passed $600M, half from AI (excerpt only) — [Forbes, December 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/annatong/2025/12/15/notion-kicks-off-employee-share-sale-at-11-billion-valuation-as-ai-accelerates-its-growth/). Estimated $865M as of July 2026, up 82% year over year; methodology paywalled — [Sacra, August 2026](https://sacra.com/research/notion-at-865m-year-growing-82-yoy/). Another Sacra page reportedly shows $610M at end-2025 and +53% (excerpt only), which does not obviously reconcile — [Sacra profile](https://sacra.com/c/notion/).
- **Obsidian (low confidence, excerpt only):** estimated 1M to 1.5M+ users and $5M–$25M ARR, unconfirmed by the company — [Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/obsidian-usage-revenue-valuation-growth-statistics). A figure of about 1.5M users and $25M is secondhand — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/obsidian-vs-notion-2026/).

Market reports:
- **[Market report, February 2026]** Note-taking apps: $11.02B in 2025, $13.3B in 2026, $28.05B by 2030 at 20.5% CAGR. Model not disclosed on the public page. Scope is broad: digital note storage revenue plus bundled goods across commercial and private users — [The Business Research Company via Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5790688/note-taking-app-market-report).
- **[Secondhand, in a vendor article]** $4.84B in 2026 to $19.63B in 2030 at 41.9% CAGR, attributed to buildin.ai. I did not check the original or its scope — [Arvor](https://arvor.co/en/news/second-brain-ai-2026).
- **[Weak]** Notion at about 25% and Obsidian at about 8% of the market, attributed to Capterra (excerpt only) — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/obsidian-vs-notion-2026/). Capterra is a review directory, so I would not use these numbers.

### Inferences
- **Sanity check:** the two report figures for 2026 differ by a factor of about 2.7 ($13.3B vs $4.84B). If Notion were at about $865M with a 25% share, the market would be about $3.5B, far from $13.3B. These numbers cannot all be right, and I would not put any total market figure on a slide without this caveat.
- Notion's growth reflects team and enterprise buying, including AI. It does not prove that individuals will pay to organize personal notes.
- **Price signal for individuals:** Obsidian's core is free, and Sync costs $4–5/month. Notion Plus is €9.50 and Mem's entry price is $9. A personal add-on likely has to justify itself near or below those levels, or next to a general AI subscription. This is an inference; I found no willingness-to-pay data.
- **Possible gap:** Notion's included agent features are on Business, not Plus. An individual on Free or Plus may therefore have no ongoing included Notion AI. This is a candidate opening, but one that Notion can close with a pricing change, and it could also mean these users are low-spending. Check it against the competitor research.

### Gaps
- No credible size or growth figure exists for second-brain, PKM or personal AI organizing as a segment.
- No Notion revenue split between individuals and teams was found.
- No willingness-to-pay data for an organizing assistant was found. The team has beta volunteers but no paying-intent evidence.
- No retention or churn data for AI note tools was found.

## Which gaps in existing tools recur in user complaints?

### Takeaway
The most supported gaps are maintenance of existing structure, working with existing tools without migration, and scattered notes, followed by privacy and transparent AI changes. Almost all of this is anecdotal, and a growing set of free tools already overlaps it, so it is a list of leads and not validated demand.

### Cited Findings
- **Upkeep and going stale:** Manual context files "silently go stale" and end-of-session discipline "almost always fails long-term" (anecdotes, 2026) — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/design-your-vault-for-ai-orientation-not-just-human-navigation/112010). PARA upkeep is described as "the tax" (vendor, July 2026) — [Iwo Szapar](https://www.iwoszapar.com/p/para-method).
- **Messy backlog and audit (supply, not demand):** Vault Audit AI, a free and open-source plugin announced in August 2026, flags orphan notes, tagging problems and overlapping topics, and runs batch actions. The page showed no replies, so it demonstrates supply, not user demand — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/vault-audit-ai-audit-and-organize-your-obsidian-vault-with-ai/117080). Note Companion (excerpt only) can create, delete, merge and organize files — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/note-companion-ai-transcription-youtube-capture-and-smart-workflows-for-obsidian/108895). File Organizer 2000 (excerpt only) suggests folders, tags and filenames — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/ai-file-organizer-2000/92950).
- **Fragmentation (anecdote, excerpt only):** One commenter finds it irritating to have notes in Apple Notes, Confluence, Notion and Google Docs — [Hacker News via search; thread uncertain](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40493176). The Korn et al. abstract also frames the problem around scattered digital environments, without quantifying it — [ECIS 2026](https://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2026/cog_hbis/cog_hbis/38/).
- **Migration (anecdotes and tool signals, excerpt only):** One reviewer wants Notion to sync with Apple Notes, where most of their notes live — [AWS Marketplace reviews](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-eoc4gcvwcs3a6?page=734). New tools bridge rather than migrate: Apple Notes Sync binds one vault folder — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/apple-notes-sync/116252). OpenKnowledge opens Obsidian vaults directly but has no Notion migration tool — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48675435).
- **Privacy (anecdote and tool signal):** One Notion reviewer asks whether Notion reads their pages (excerpt only) — [AWS Marketplace reviews](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-eoc4gcvwcs3a6?page=734). A commenter uses local models to query past notes — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1oth/i_deleted_my_second_brain). Vault Audit AI supports Ollama for local operation, and otherwise sends content to remote providers — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/vault-audit-ai-audit-and-organize-your-obsidian-vault-with-ai/117080).
- **Trust in changes and writing back (anecdote):** One user says reading from Obsidian into Claude works very well, but writing back through MCP tools is unreliable — [Obsidian Forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/design-your-vault-for-ai-orientation-not-just-human-navigation/112010). Notion AI reviews mention inconsistency and inaccuracy — [AWS Marketplace reviews](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-eoc4gcvwcs3a6?page=734).
- **Thinking and use, still unsolved (practitioner, older):** Note apps were found to improve storage and not thinking (2023) — [Platformer](https://platformer.substack.com/p/why-note-taking-apps-dont-make-us). This is the area the team moved away from, because it found no demand.

### Inferences
Ranking by strength of evidence, not by market size:
1. **Backlog and upkeep without migration.** Best fit with Oracle's concept: start from the existing Notion workspace, propose changes and keep it current. Risk: free Obsidian plugins already cover this on that platform; whether a comparable Notion-native option exists belongs to the competitor research.
2. **Reviewable, reversible AI changes.** Plausibly needed for write access to personal notes, and also a cheap differentiator. Evidence is thin.
3. **Cross-app fragmentation.** Recurring complaint, but it conflicts with a Notion-only MVP and is much harder to build.
4. **Privacy and local processing.** Clear in technical communities, but sending Notion content to a third-party AI is an additional trust hurdle. Strength among mainstream Notion users is unknown.

Verdict on the team's hypothesis:
- **Supported:** some users do struggle with placement, and PARA has specific, automatable friction. Interest in AI categorization exists. Upkeep failure is a recurring theme.
- **Not supported:** that organizing is the main pain for the majority of users; there is no quantitative evidence.
- **Contradicted or under pressure:** vendors and some users argue AI search makes organizing unnecessary, older email research points the same way, and critics blame filing itself as a distraction from real work. Mem, a product built on automatic organization, has repositioned, and free tools are appearing.
- **Sharper hypothesis for the team to test:** "Notion users with a large and messy workspace who already tried to maintain a system and fell behind will approve AI-suggested cleanup, and this improves their later retrieval or use more than AI search alone." Suggested tests: run a messy-versus-organized retrieval comparison, measure suggestion acceptance rate on real workspaces, and ask for a concrete payment commitment.
- Users who are satisfied with simple files or a daily routine should be treated as non-target, not as evidence of demand. The team's evidence (three Reddit users, plus Notion contacts) remains small even with this research.

### Gaps
- Direct Reddit complaint frequency could not be measured because the tools block Reddit. A manual review of the named subreddits by the team would close this gap; count posts by theme and record the search terms and dates.
- No Notion-specific evidence on fears about third-party AI access to workspaces was found.
- The fragmentation quote's exact Hacker News thread could not be confirmed from the search output.
- Whether non-technical users share these complaints is unknown, as the available communities skew technical.
